01

Overview

A spoken conversation carries more than words: rain, a car horn or a crying baby in the background. We ask whether audio LLMs keep that environmental context across turns, and if not, where it gets lost.

TL;DR

On EnvMem, acoustic memory decays faster than semantic memory as dialogues grow. Yet the sound remains linearly decodable from deep hidden states even on failed trials. Failures come from representational trajectory drift, not from erased information or misallocated attention, and patching in a clean representation rescues most of them.

RQ1

How large is the gap?

What is the performance gap between linguistic and environmental acoustic memory in multi-turn LALMs?

RQ2

Where does it come from?

Does acoustic memory failure stem from the representation level (information lost across layers) or the retrieval level (too little attention to early acoustic cues)?

Abstract

Large audio language models (LALMs) process both speech and environmental acoustic cues, yet struggle to retain non-speech information across multi-turn interactions. The performance gap between semantic (speech) and acoustic (non-speech) understanding remains poorly understood, and the underlying mechanisms of representation and retrieval are still unclear. This work introduces EnvMem, a controlled multi-turn benchmark designed to study this gap and identify the root causes of failures at the representation (i.e., latent embeddings) and retrieval levels (i.e., attention allocation). We further conduct post-hoc interventions to probe representational structure and attention dynamics. Our results reveal representational trajectory drift as the key failure mode, while showing that attention allocation tested here do not consistently explain the degradation. Overall, we provide a systematic framework for analyzing and improving non-linguistic memory in long-context LALMs, shedding light on future data and training design for robust acoustic memory modeling.

The EnvMem benchmark

The environmental sound appears only in the first user turn. Filler turns extend the context without new sounds, and the final turn asks about either the sound or a fact from that same first turn, so both probes share one audio context.

Controlled by design

Sounds come from 10 ESC-50 classes in four families (weather/water, machine, vocal-bio, impulse), mixing continuous textures with transient events. Dialogues are GPT-4o templates over eight everyday topics; 38 of 208 candidate templates were removed by a 42-word lexicon that blocks textual leakage of the sound.

Every utterance is synthesized with one TTS voice (Kokoro af_heart, 16 kHz) to remove speaker confounds. 10 classes × 4 context lengths × 50 templates gives 2,000 acoustic and 2,000 semantic probes. Models: Qwen2.5-Omni, Kimi-Audio and Qwen2-Audio, all with white-box access.

04

Diagnosing the failure

Two hypotheses could explain the gap. Either the sound is lost as hidden states propagate (representation), or it is kept but the model fails to attend to it (retrieval). We test both observationally, then causally. Main analyses use Qwen2.5-Omni.

1

Not erasedThe sound stays decodable in deep layers, even when the model answers wrong.

A linear probe on the final query token recovers the 10-way sound class from layer 20 onward. At N = 16 it reaches about 70% by layer 25 (about 80% at N = 2). Restricted to trials the model failed, layer-25 probe accuracy is still about 48%, far above the 10% chance level.

Context length also moves where the sound is integrated: at N = 2 the probe reaches about 0.50 by layer 5, while at N = 8 and 16 mid-layer accuracy stays near 0.20 up to layer 15 and jumps in layers 16–21.

2

DriftFailed trajectories collapse toward a short-context regime.

Cross-layer CKA compares hidden states of failed long-context trials with successful trials of the same N and with short (N = 2) trials, matched on sound class. Layers 0–5 show no differentiation. In layers 6–19, failed trials are consistently closer to the short baseline than to successful ones: δ = CKA(failed, short) − CKA(failed, success) is 0.054, 0.055 and 0.067 at N = 4, 8, 16. In layers 20–28 a high-similarity block appears but peaks off the diagonal, a phase-shifted integration.

We call this representational trajectory drift: the information is present, but its format no longer matches what the decoding pathway expects.

3

Not attentionAttention allocation does not explain the failure.

If long contexts diluted attention, the anchor turn should receive less attention as N grows. Instead, at N = 4 the anchor gets less attention than fillers through most layers, while at N = 16 it gets more (+0.005 to +0.010). Failed trials are also no more diffuse than successful ones: the turns needed to cover 90% of attention differ by only +0.04, −0.10 and −0.10.

4

CausalRestoring the representation rescues failed predictions; editing attention does not.

Activation patching. On failed trials we overwrite the decoding-position hidden state at layer 25 with a donor vector. For Qwen2.5-Omni at N = 16, a clean same-class donor lifts accuracy from 0.13 to 0.75, while a wrong-class donor (0.17) and the model's own contaminated vector (0.22) stay near baseline. Qwen2-Audio shows the same pattern (clean donors 0.30–0.45).

Attention masking. Amplifying the anchor turn, suppressing fillers, or a magnitude-matched random boost all change accuracy within noise; every 95% CI includes zero. A sweep over layer offsets and boost sizes peaks at +3.7, against +1.3 for the random control.

Robustness

The effect is not tied to one mixing level or one model.

Across models. Kimi-Audio and Qwen2-Audio show the same delayed acoustic integration at N ≥ 8: the sound is barely decodable in early and middle layers, then recovers sharply in the deepest blocks.

06

What this means

Acoustic information is largely preserved but becomes functionally misaligned with decoding in long contexts.

Benchmarks

Separate sound from speech

Holding scripts, voice and turn structure fixed while only the probe target changes isolates modality-specific memory from content recall.

Bottleneck

Representation, not retrieval

Pre- and post-training that under-emphasize acoustic events, and weakly couple them with semantics, likely drive the gap.

Mitigation

Train for format-compatible memory

Post-hoc patching shows the failure is correctable; the next step is integrating acoustic representations during training.
